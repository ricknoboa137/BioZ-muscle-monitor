BioZ Muscle Monitor — Schematic, Rev B

Portable tetrapolar bioimpedance spectroscopy monitor for biceps fatigue and fluid shift. Reverse-engineered from an unpublished 2026 undergraduate thesis (student, supervisors and institution withheld), and corrected against manufacturer datasheets.

  • AFE MAX30009ENA+ · 25-WLP 0.4 mm
  • MCU nRF54L15-QFAA-R · QFN48
  • Store CY15V108QI · 8 Mb F-RAM
  • Sweep 20 pts · 5 kHz–250 kHz · 1 s
  • Drive 128 µARMS
  • Rails 2.5 V · 1.8 VA · 1.8 VD
  • Cell LP103048JU 3.7 V 1.43 Ah
  • Runtime 29 d continuous / ~130 d typical
  • Board 50 × 44 mm · 4 layer

Read before using any pin number on this drawing

Verified against the manufacturer datasheet, pin-by-pin: MAX30009ENA+ (all 25 bumps), nRF54L15-QFAA QFN48 (all 49 pins), CY15V108QI 8-GQFN (all 8 pins, read off the package figure by coordinate, not by text order), BQ24073RGT VQFN-16 (all 16 pins + thermal pad, plus KISET and KILIM), SN74LVC1G14 SC70-5 (from the KiCad 10 official symbol library).

Drawn by pin name because I could not reach the datasheet: MAX77827 — analog.com and maximintegrated.com both time out from this machine and Mouser blocks automated fetches. Pin numbers 1–6, 8–11, 13, 14 come from the KiCad 10 Regulator_Switching official symbol; pins 7 and 12 are not modelled there and are unknown to me. The RSEL = 634 kΩ → 2.5 V mapping is verified, from the MAX77827 EV-kit datasheet Table 2. ADPL40502 — 6-lead LFCSP pin numbering not obtained; drawn as VIN/VOUT/GND/EN/NC/EP. J2 SWD header and the antenna ladder node order are drawn by function, not by verified number.

Every one of these is listed with its resolution in Verify before you route. Do not commit a footprint or a netlist for those parts without closing that list first.

Safety — this device passes current through a person

  • Battery-powered and galvanically floating is the safe topology. Nothing in this design may be referenced to mains. The single thing most likely to break that is the SWD header: connecting a mains-powered debug probe while electrodes are on a subject creates a mains-referenced path to skin. Never program or debug the board while it is attached to a person.
  • Stimulus is limited by two independent mechanisms — the 47 nF DC-blocking capacitors in both drive leads (C5, C6) and the MAX30009's internal current-range resistor. DRVXR is left unconnected specifically so that BIOZ_EXT_RES = 0 and the part keeps its automatic, frequency-dependent stimulus limiting. Do not fit an external REXT without re-doing the patient-current analysis.
  • Patient auxiliary current limits rise with frequency above 1 kHz. The 128 µARMS drive is why the sweep floor is 5 kHz and not 16 Hz. The exact limit table must be read out of IEC 60601-1 — I have not read it and the numbers here are not a compliance argument.
  • Lithium cell. Use a protected LP103048JU (with PCM) for prototypes — do not build your own protection. Charge rate is set to 0.28 C, deliberately below the thesis's 0.56 C, for thermal margin inside a sealed IP54 box.
  • Human-subject research needs ethics / IRB approval before any data collection, independent of whether the electronics work.
  • Any medical claim invokes IEC 60601-1. That is a different engineering process with its own risk file, essential-performance definition and type testing — not a checklist bolted onto this design.

Sheet 1 — Power input, charging and rails

SHEET 1/4 Title Power & charging Rev B Rails VBAT → VSYS → 2.5 V → 1.8 VA / 1.8 VD Gnd GNDD, GNDA joined at R1

Sheet 1 notes

  • Why a buck-boost and not a buck. The 1.8 V LDOs need 1.95 V at their input (1.8 V + 150 mV dropout). A depleted lithium cell reaches 3.0 V and VSYS follows it, so a buck alone would still work — but the MAX77827 keeps 2.5 V constant across the whole cell range and through the 4.4 V charging excursion, which means the LDO dropout, the LC filter corner and the PSRR are all fixed operating points rather than functions of battery state. That is worth the part on an instrument.
  • VSYS is 4.4 V while charging, ≈ VBAT otherwise. BQ24073 (unlike BQ24072) regulates OUT to a fixed 4.4 V. The battery-sense divider therefore reads 4.4 V, not cell voltage, whenever a charger is attached — firmware must gate the low-battery logic on /PGOOD.
  • The NTC measures the board, not the cell. The LP103048JU with PCM has two wires, so U9 is a discrete 103AT-2 on the PCB. Place it against the cell. It still does the job that matters here — inhibiting charge when the sealed enclosure is hot — but it is not a true pack thermistor.
  • Thermal. Worst case PD(U8) = (5.0−4.4)(0.41) + (4.4−3.0)(0.40) = 0.806 W, TJ = 40 + 44.5 × 0.806 = 75.9 °C. The BQ2407x also has an internal thermal-regulation loop that folds back charge current, so this is self-protecting rather than a cliff.

Sheet 2 — MCU, radio and debug

SHEET 2/4 Title nRF54L15 · BLE · SWD Rev B Rail V1P8D via FL1 Pinout DATASHEET VERIFIED, ALL 49 PINS

Sheet 2 notes

  • Why 1.8 V and not 2.5 V. Correction 10. The nRF54L15 operates from 1.7–3.6 V, so 1.8 V is in range — but note it sits only 100 mV above the minimum, so the LDO's initial accuracy and load regulation must be checked (see verify list). In exchange, every logic interface in the system is at one level with proper margin, and the MCU rail is independent of battery state.
  • DC/DC. VREGMAIN starts in LDO mode and firmware enables DC/DC via VREGMAIN.DCDCEN; the device probes for the inductor on DCC and stays in LDO mode if it does not find one, reporting via VREGMAIN.INDUCTORDET. So fitting L3 is safe either way.
  • Absolute maximum ratings are unavailable. Nordic's datasheet v0.7 lists supply voltage, I/O pin voltage, and ESD ratings as TBD. I cannot state an nRF54L15 absolute maximum, and I have not. Get the production specification before relying on any overvoltage margin.
  • The two RF grounding notes are not optional. Nordic states C19's ground must connect only to pin 32 on the top layer, and pin 32 must connect only to pin 49 under the package; and C20's ground must be isolated from every ground layer except the bottom one. These are harmonic-filtering requirements and they are layout constraints, not schematic ones — they are carried into the PCB brief.

The interesting number in that table

Idle current is 311 µA, of which 290 µA is the digital LDO's own quiescent current. The entire MCU, radio and memory together account for 20 µA. If runtime ever needs improving, the lever is the LDO — not the firmware. A nano-quiescent LDO (sub-10 µA IQ) would take idle from 0.27 mA to about 0.03 mA and push typical runtime past a year. I have kept the ADPL40502 on both rails because it is the part the source design verified and it keeps the BOM to one LDO type, but this is the first thing to revisit.

The bottleneck is not any bus — it is filter settling

Every data path above is idle more than 99.9 % of the time. The constraint that actually decides whether a 1 s sweep is achievable is the BioZ low-pass filter settling time at each of the 20 frequency steps. The sweep gives 50 ms per point. A narrow DLPF setting (tens of Hz) takes a substantial fraction of that to settle after a frequency change, and any residue appears as a systematic magnitude and phase error that looks exactly like a physiological signal.

Design the sweep around this, not around the SPI. Either widen the DLPF during the sweep and accept the noise penalty (there is 60× of margin to spend), or reduce the number of frequencies, or lengthen the sweep period. Measure the step response on the bench before trusting a 1 s number.

7. What will most likely stop this board working

1 — The buck-boost switching at 2.5 MHz next to a 128 µA measurement

The sense signal is 6.4 mVRMS and the converter switches 1.6 A peak in a loop a few millimetres away, inside a 50 × 44 mm board. That is roughly 108 dB of separation to find. The LC filter gives 40 dB at the switching fundamental, the LDO's PSRR gives about 70 dB more at low frequency but degrades badly above 100 kHz — right where the top of the sweep lives. The residual path is not conducted, it is the magnetic loop of L8 and its input and output capacitors coupling into the electrode traces.
Mitigation: keep the L8 / C29 / C9 loop area minimal and entirely under the Würth shield frame; keep the electrode nets on the opposite side of R1 with an unbroken GNDA reference under them; and — the cheap one — pick a sweep frequency set that avoids 2.5 MHz / n. None of the 20 points should land within a few kHz of 2.5 MHz ÷ 10 = 250 kHz. Move the top point to 240 kHz.

2 — R5/R6 rolling off the top of the sweep

40.2 kΩ against ~10 pF is a pole near 400 kHz. Two things follow. The magnitude error at the top of the band is systematic, and — worse — the 1 % tolerance on R5 and R6 makes the two sense paths mismatched, which converts into a phase error that the CAL port cannot remove, because the calibration path goes through CAL1–CAL4 and bypasses R5/R6 entirely. This is the one that will quietly corrupt data rather than stop the board working.
Mitigation: the sweep ceiling is set to 250 kHz, and R5/R6 are specified as a matched pair (0.1 % if available in 0603, otherwise same reel and same orientation). If the band must reach 500 kHz, drop R5/R6 to 10 kΩ and re-derive the single-fault patient current from the 1.8 V rail.

3 — The 0.4 mm pitch WLP

The MAX30009 is a 25-bump WLP on 0.4 mm pitch in a 2.03 mm square. It forces via-in-pad with filled and capped microvias, and it puts AGND (C3), DGND (E1), AVDD (C1) and DVDD (D1) within 0.4 mm of each other while requiring them to be on two different ground pours joined only at R1. Getting that escape wrong is a respin.
Mitigation: confirm the fab's capability class before the stackup is fixed, not after — this is called out as its own section in the PCB brief.

4 — Charging heat inside a sealed IP54 box next to a lithium cell

Even at the reduced 400 mA the charger dissipates 0.8 W into an enclosure with a thermal resistance of 0.83 °C/W and no ventilation, with the cell sitting beside it.
Mitigation: the NTC must be physically against the cell, not next to the charger, or it will report the charger's heat and throttle for the wrong reason. Give U8's thermal pad the largest copper area and via count on the board, and keep it at the opposite end from the battery.
