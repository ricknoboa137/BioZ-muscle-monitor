# 1. Purpose and scope

This dossier specifies a battery-powered, body-worn bioimpedance spectroscopy instrument that measures the electrical impedance of the biceps to track muscle fatigue and fluid shift during and after exercise. It is a corrected reproduction of the device described in an unpublished 2026 undergraduate thesis from a Hungarian technical university's electrical engineering programme, cited here without identifying the student, supervisors, or institution.

The source design is architecturally sound and is reproduced faithfully. What has changed is a list of sixteen defects: the eight supplied with the brief, and eight more found while verifying pinouts and re-running the numbers. Two of the eight I found are hard faults that would stop the board working. Each correction is identified against the original in section 5.

B Scope of this document: requirements, signal budget, part selection, power budget, data rates, sheet-by-sheet connectivity, complete net list, layout rules and a handoff to PCB layout.
B Out of scope: firmware, mechanical design of the enclosure beyond fit, clinical validation, and any regulatory submission.

# 3. Signal reality — the numbers that constrain everything

Everything downstream falls out of these figures. They are stated first because getting them wrong is cheaper to find here than after a board spin.

|# Quantity | Value | What it forces
| Segment impedance, biceps tetrapolar | 20 to 80 ohm | Sets drive level and required resolution
| Change to be resolved | approx 50 milliohm | Literature: R rises 9-10 percent at 60-100 percent MVC. Seeing 1 percent of that on 50 ohm needs 50 milliohm
| MAX30009 repeatability | 0.80 milliohm RMS at 25 ohm | Datasheet. Gives about 60x margin. The AFE is not the limiting element
| Drive current | 128 microamp RMS | BIOZ_IDRV_RGE 0x3, BIOZ_VDRV_MAG 0x0, giving 181 microamp peak
| Sense voltage developed | 6.4 mV RMS across 50 ohm | Comfortably inside the 1000 mV peak-to-peak differential input range
| Sweep floor | 5 kHz | Two independent reasons — see below
| Sweep ceiling | 250 kHz | Set by R5 and R6, not by the AFE — see below
| Sweep step | ratio 1.23 per point | 50 to the power 1/19, bracketing the muscle beta-dispersion at 30-80 kHz

## Why the sweep floor is 5 kHz and not 16 Hz

Two independent constraints land in the same place.

N The DC-blocking capacitors C5 and C6 present 677 ohm at 5 kHz. The drive amplifier swings at most 500 mV peak, and 181 microamp peak into 677 ohm is 123 mV, which fits. At 500 Hz the same capacitor is 6.8 kohm and the drive would clip.
N Patient auxiliary current limits fall toward 100 microamp below about 1 kHz. A 128 microamp RMS stimulus is only defensible above that. The exact limit table must be read out of IEC 60601-1; it has not been read here.

## Why the sweep ceiling is 250 kHz and not 500 kHz

The MAX30009 itself reaches 500 kHz. The limit is the series patient-protection resistors. R5 and R6 at 40.2 kohm working into an assumed 10 pF of input plus track capacitance form a pole at about 400 kHz. At 250 kHz the magnitude error is a few percent and correctable; at 500 kHz it is not.

Worse than the magnitude error is the mismatch. R5 and R6 are 1 percent parts, so the two sense paths have slightly different poles, which appears as a differential phase error. The calibration port cannot remove it, because calibration runs through CAL1 to CAL4 and bypasses R5 and R6 entirely. This is design risk 2 in section 12.

# 4. Architecture and part selection

## 4.1 Signal chain

The device drives a known sinusoidal current into the outer electrode pair, measures the resulting differential voltage on the inner pair, and computes complex impedance from simultaneous in-phase and quadrature ADC results. A precision 680 ohm resistor on a four-wire calibration port provides the absolute accuracy reference.

An integrated AFE was chosen over a discrete excitation, filter and gain chain. The reasoning in the source design holds: lower circuit complexity, better SNR, shorter development time and lower project risk.

## 4.2 Part selection and alternatives

|# Function | Chosen part | Deciding parameter | Why this one

| Digital LDO | ADPL40502ACPZ-1.8 | Same part, second instance | New in Rev B. Keeps F-RAM switching current off the analog rail. Same part number keeps the BOM to one LDO type
| Antenna | Wurth WE-MCA 74889302450 | Pre-characterised 2.4 GHz chip antenna | Matches Nordic's reference matching network. Avoids a custom antenna and its characterisation cost

Note on certification: the radio is an integrated SoC, not a pre-certified module. That is a deliberate cost and size trade the source design made, and it is retained. It means full radiated emissions and intentional-radiator testing falls on this design rather than being inherited from a module's certification. If the project is heading for market rather than research use, substituting a pre-certified BLE module removes a substantial test programme, at the cost of board area.

# 5. Corrections made to the source design

Sixteen corrections. Items 1 to 8 are the defects supplied with the brief; items 9 to 16 were found during datasheet verification and re-running the numbers.

|# No | Source design | Revision B | Why
| 1 | MAX30009 VREF 1 uF returns to GNDD | Returns to GNDA | Datasheet VREF pin description: connect a 1 uF X5R capacitor between VREF and AGND. Referencing the ADC reference buffer to digital ground on an absolute-accuracy instrument is a direct accuracy and noise risk
| 2 | DVDD has 0.1 uF only | 0.1 uF and 10 uF, C3 plus new C42 | Datasheet DVDD pin: bypass to DGND with a 0.1 uF AND a 10 uF X5R capacitor. AVDD already had both, C4 plus C2
| 3 | DRVXR shorted to DRVSJ | DRVXR left unconnected | Neither legal option. Internal current setting chosen: with BIOZ_EXT_RES equal to 0 the datasheet says the pin should be unconnected, and stimulus magnitude is then automatically limited with frequency for patient safety. An external resistor disables that clamp. Absolute accuracy comes from the R7 calibration port, not from R_EXT, so nothing is given up
| 4 | F-RAM VDD undecoupled | C43 100 nF plus C44 1 uF at pin 8 | A memory switching at 20 MHz with no local decoupling injects its full switching current into the rail
| 5 | BOM item 37 lists R13, R18, R18 for quantity 3 | R13, R17, R18 — three distinct designators | Designator collision left one 1.5 kohm resistor unaccounted for
| 6a | R4 348 ohm but part number is a 232 ohm part | R4 200 ohm, RC0603FR-07200RL | Value and part mismatch; value changed again with the LED rework, correction 13
| 6b | C22 10 nF but part number is the 1.5 pF GJM0335C1E1R5WB01D | C22 10 nF, GRM033R71A103KA01D | Value and part mismatch. C22 is the DECA capacitor and 10 nF is correct per Nordic's reference circuit
| 6c | C5 to C7 47 nF but part number is a 4.7 uF part | 47 nF, C0805C473K5RACTU | Value and part mismatch. Safety-relevant: a 4.7 uF part in the drive leads would raise single-fault AC leakage by 100x
| 6d | R11 210 kohm but the calculation asks 220 kohm | R11 220 kohm | Value follows the calculation. Thresholds still need recomputing at 1.8 V, see V6
| 7 | Charge 800 mA, 0.56 C, junction 86.3 degC | 400 mA, 0.28 C, R14 2.21 kohm | Recomputed worst case as 0.806 W giving 75.9 degC, a 10 degC improvement in a sealed box beside a lithium cell. Costs about 4.5 h charge time on a device charged every three weeks
| 8 | F-RAM DNU pin 7 tied to GNDD | DNU left floating | Datasheet: either leave floating or tie to VDD. Ground is not one of the permitted options
| 9 | BQ24073 EN1 and EN2 both tied high | EN2 high, EN1 to GND | HARD FAULT. Datasheet table 7-2: EN2 equal 1 with EN1 equal 1 is Standby, USB suspend mode. Resistor-programmed ILIM mode requires EN2 equal 1 and EN1 equal 0. As drawn the source design would never charge the battery
| 10 | 2.5 V MCU rail driving 1.8 V peripherals | Unified 1.8 V digital domain | HARD FAULT, two independent violations. CY15V108QI absolute maximum input is VDD plus 0.5 V equal 2.39 V, so 2.5 V logic on CS, SCK, SI and WP is over the absolute maximum. And nRF54L15 V_IH is 0.7 times VDD equal 1.75 V at 2.5 V while the AFE SDO and INT drive only 1.8 V, a 50 mV logic margin. Running the MCU at 1.8 V fixes both with no translators
| 11 | One 1.8 V LDO feeds AFE and F-RAM | Two LDOs, U10 to the AFE and U14 to MCU plus F-RAM | Follows from 10 and independently right: the F-RAM 1.3 mA of 20 MHz switching current was landing on the analog supply of a precision impedance meter. Both MAX30009 supplies stay on U10 because AVDD-to-DVDD absolute maximum is only plus or minus 0.3 V
| 12 | Battery divider 10 kohm over 1 kohm | 1 Mohm over 100 kohm plus C36 100 nF | Same 1 in 11 ratio so the 3.1 V threshold code 640 is unchanged, but 3.4 microamp instead of 336 microamp. The original divider silently consumed 12 percent of the whole power budget
| 13 | White LED, quoted V_F 1.8 V, high-side from 2.5 V | Red LED, low-side from 2.5 V, R4 200 ohm | A white LED needs about 2.9 V and cannot run off a 2.5 V rail at all, and the GPIO is now 1.8 V so high-side drive is impossible. Low-side works with no extra parts. Logic is inverted, low equals lit
| 14 | Pin 47 unaccounted for; DECRF and DECA separate; DECD 2.2 nF; no bulk | Pin 47 is VDD; DECRF tied to DECA; DECD 10 nF; C13 10 uF bulk added | Datasheet pin table: pin 47 is VDD, and DECRF carries the explicit note that it must be connected to DECA. Nordic's reference circuit puts 10 nF on DECD and adds a 10 uF bulk capacitor
| 15 | BQ24073 TMR tied to GND | TMR left unconnected | Datasheet: TMR to VSS disables all safety timers. Floating selects the default timers. On a lithium charger in a sealed enclosure, keeping the safety timer is the right default
| 16 | Slide switch breaks the main supply; FPWM hard-tied | Switch drives MAX77827 EN; FPWM to MCU P0.03 | Switching the converter enable instead of its output current keeps 500 mA out of a slide switch and drops the off state to converter quiescent current. A GPIO on FPWM lets firmware force fixed-frequency PWM only during a sweep and use skip mode the rest of the time

Carried forward unchanged because verification showed them to be correct: the full MAX30009 bump map; R7 680 ohm 0.1 percent in a true four-wire Kelvin connection; C7 47 nF between DRVXC and DRVSJ; TRIG to DGND and FCLK unconnected; R15 634 kohm for 2.5 V; K_ISET 890 and K_ILIM 1550; the entire antenna matching network, which matches Nordic's reference circuit component-for-component; and the LC filter mathematics, where Q_S equal 0.49 and f_c equal 244 kHz both check out exactly.

## 6.1 Sheet 1 — Power input, charging and rails

I img/sheet-01.png | Sheet 1 of 4 — input protection, BQ24073 charger and power path, MAX77827 buck-boost to 2.5 V, damped LC filter, and the two 1.8 V LDOs. Red is power, blue is analog, black is signal. Triangle markers reference the correction table in section 5.

The power chain is battery, then charger with power path, then buck-boost to a fixed 2.5 V, then a damped LC filter, then two parallel LDOs producing separate analog and digital 1.8 V rails.

B The buck-boost holds 2.5 V constant across the whole cell range and through the 4.4 V charging excursion. That makes LDO dropout, the filter corner and PSRR fixed operating points rather than functions of battery state, which matters on an instrument.
B V_SYS is 4.4 V while charging and approximately V_BAT otherwise. BQ24073, unlike BQ24072, regulates OUT to a fixed 4.4 V. The battery-sense divider therefore reads 4.4 V whenever a charger is attached, so firmware must gate the low-battery logic on the PGOOD signal.
B The NTC measures the board, not the cell. The LP103048JU with PCM has two wires, so U9 is a discrete 103AT-2 on the PCB. Place it against the cell. It still inhibits charging when the sealed enclosure is hot, which is the risk that matters, but it is not a true pack thermistor.
B Worst-case charger dissipation is 0.6 V times 0.41 A plus 1.4 V times 0.40 A, giving 0.806 W and a junction temperature of 75.9 degC at 40 degC ambient. The BQ2407x also has an internal thermal regulation loop that folds back charge current, so this is self-protecting rather than a cliff.

! THE INTERESTING NUMBER IN THAT TABLE. Idle current is 311 microamp, of which 290 microamp is the digital LDO's own quiescent current. The entire MCU, radio and memory together account for 20 microamp. If runtime ever needs improving, the lever is the LDO, not the firmware. A nano-quiescent LDO with sub-10 microamp quiescent would take idle from 0.27 mA to about 0.03 mA and push typical runtime past a year. The ADPL40502 is kept on both rails because it is the part the source design verified and it holds the BOM to one LDO type, but this is the first thing to revisit.

| F-RAM capacity used in 2 h | 864 kB | 1024 kB | 84 percent
| MAX30009 FIFO headroom | 40 entries/s | 256 entries | 6.4 s to overflow
| BLE offload of a full record, 2 Mbps PHY | 864 kB | about 250 kbps application | about 28 s

! THE BOTTLENECK IS NOT ANY BUS — IT IS FILTER SETTLING. Every data path above is idle more than 99.9 percent of the time. What actually decides whether a 1 s sweep is achievable is the BioZ low-pass filter settling time at each of the 20 frequency steps. The sweep allows 50 ms per point. A narrow DLPF setting of tens of hertz takes a substantial fraction of that to settle after a frequency change, and any residue appears as a systematic magnitude and phase error that looks exactly like a physiological signal. Design the sweep around this, not around the SPI. Either widen the DLPF during the sweep and accept the noise penalty, since there is 60x of margin to spend, or reduce the number of frequencies, or lengthen the sweep period. Measure the step response on the bench before trusting a 1 s number.

# 10. Layout rules

Full detail is in pcb-brief.md. The rules that originate in the schematic rather than the layout are:

B Four layers: Top for components and signals, an inner solid ground layer, an inner power layer with voltage islands, and Bottom for signals.
B 20 mil for power and high-current tracks, 10 mil for signals.
B Board outline 50 by 44 mm.
B Analog and digital ground pours are separate and joined at a single point, R1, a 0 ohm link placed directly under the MAX30009 at the AGND to DGND bump corner.
B No copper pour under or around the chip antenna, on any layer.
B Wurth SMD shield frame, 26 by 26 mm, over the power section.
B The MAX30009 is a 0.4 mm pitch WLP. This forces via-in-pad with filled and capped microvias and therefore fixes the fabrication class before the stackup can be finalised.
B C19 ground connects only to nRF54L15 pin 32 on the top layer, and pin 32 connects only to pin 49 under the package.
B C20 ground must be isolated from every ground layer except the bottom one.
B The L8, C29 and C9 switching loop must be minimal in area and sit entirely under the shield frame.

# 12. Warnings

## 12.1 Safety

! THIS DEVICE PASSES CURRENT THROUGH A PERSON. Battery-powered and galvanically floating is the safe topology and nothing in this design may be referenced to mains. The single thing most likely to break that is the SWD header: connecting a mains-powered debug probe while electrodes are on a subject creates a mains-referenced path to skin. NEVER PROGRAM OR DEBUG THE BOARD WHILE IT IS ATTACHED TO A PERSON.

! STIMULUS LIMITING. Current into the body is limited by two independent mechanisms: the 47 nF DC-blocking capacitors in both drive leads, C5 and C6, and the MAX30009 internal current-range resistor. DRVXR is left unconnected specifically so that BIOZ_EXT_RES stays 0 and the part keeps its automatic frequency-dependent stimulus limiting. Do not fit an external R_EXT without redoing the patient-current analysis. Note also correction 6c: the source BOM specified a 4.7 uF part where 47 nF was intended, which would have raised single-fault AC leakage by a factor of 100.

! PATIENT AUXILIARY CURRENT. The limits rise with frequency above 1 kHz. The 128 microamp RMS drive is why the sweep floor is 5 kHz and not 16 Hz. The exact limit table must be read out of IEC 60601-1; it has not been read here and the numbers in this dossier are not a compliance argument.

! LITHIUM CELL. Use a protected LP103048JU with PCM for prototypes. Do not build your own protection circuitry. Charge rate is set to 0.28 C, deliberately below the source design's 0.56 C, for thermal margin inside a sealed IP54 box. The NTC must be placed physically against the cell, not next to the charger, or it will report the charger's own heat and throttle for the wrong reason.

! HUMAN-SUBJECT RESEARCH. Ethics or institutional review board approval is normally required before any data collection, independent of whether the electronics work.

! MEDICAL CLAIMS. Any medical claim invokes IEC 60601-1. That is a different engineering process with its own risk file, essential-performance definition and type testing. It is not a checklist bolted onto this design.

! MAINS. Nothing in this design connects to mains. If a mains-powered charger is used, it must be a certified supply with reinforced isolation, and the magnetic charge connector must never be mated while electrodes are on a subject.

## 12.2 Design risks — what will most likely stop this board working

### Risk 1 — The buck-boost switching at 2.5 MHz next to a 128 microamp measurement

The sense signal is 6.4 mV RMS and the converter switches 1.6 A peak in a loop a few millimetres away inside a 50 by 44 mm board. That is roughly 108 dB of separation to find. The LC filter gives 40 dB at the switching fundamental and the LDO PSRR gives about 70 dB more at low frequency, but PSRR degrades badly above 100 kHz, which is exactly where the top of the sweep lives. The residual path is not conducted, it is the magnetic loop of L8 and its input and output capacitors coupling into the electrode traces.

Mitigation: keep the L8, C29 and C9 loop area minimal and entirely under the Wurth shield frame; keep the electrode nets on the opposite side of R1 with an unbroken GNDA reference beneath them; and, the cheap one, choose a sweep frequency set that avoids 2.5 MHz divided by an integer. None of the 20 points should land within a few kilohertz of 250 kHz. Move the top point to 240 kHz.

### Risk 2 — R5 and R6 rolling off the top of the sweep

40.2 kohm against about 10 pF is a pole near 400 kHz. Two things follow. The magnitude error at the top of the band is systematic, and, worse, the 1 percent tolerance on R5 and R6 makes the two sense paths mismatched, which converts into a differential phase error that the calibration port cannot remove, because calibration runs through CAL1 to CAL4 and bypasses R5 and R6 entirely. This is the one that will quietly corrupt data rather than stop the board working.

Mitigation: the sweep ceiling is set to 250 kHz, and R5 and R6 are specified as a matched pair, 0.1 percent if available in 0603, otherwise same reel and same orientation. If the band must reach 500 kHz, drop R5 and R6 to 10 kohm and re-derive the single-fault patient current from the 1.8 V rail.

### Risk 3 — The 0.4 mm pitch WLP

The MAX30009 is a 25-bump WLP on 0.4 mm pitch in a 2.03 mm square. It forces via-in-pad with filled and capped microvias, and it puts AGND, DGND, AVDD and DVDD within 0.4 mm of each other while requiring them to sit on two different ground pours joined only at R1. Getting that escape wrong is a respin.

Mitigation: confirm the fabricator's capability class before the stackup is fixed, not after. This is called out as its own section in the PCB brief.

### Risk 4 — Charging heat inside a sealed box next to a lithium cell

Even at the reduced 400 mA the charger dissipates 0.8 W into an enclosure with a thermal resistance of 0.83 degC per watt and no ventilation, with the cell beside it.

Mitigation: place the NTC against the cell, give U8's thermal pad the largest copper area and via count on the board, and keep it at the opposite end from the battery.

# 13. Build sequence

N Close verify items V1 to V5 before ordering any footprint or fabrication. V1, the two unknown MAX77827 pins, is a hard blocker on the power section.
N Fabricate and assemble the power section only: U8, U7, the LC filter, U10 and U14. Do not fit U1, U5 or IC1.
N Bring up on a bench supply at the magnetic connector input, not a battery. Confirm 2.5 V at V2P5F, 1.8 V at V1P8A and V1P8D, and measure ripple at V2P5F with a short-ground probe. Expect 40 dB of attenuation at 2.5 MHz.
N Confirm charging actually starts. This is the specific check for correction 9: with EN1 low and EN2 high, verify current into the cell and that the CHG pin pulls low. If it does not charge, EN1 and EN2 are the first place to look.
N Fit U5 and IC1. Program through SWD with no electrodes connected. Confirm the SPI bus enumerates the F-RAM device ID.
N Verify the 1.8 V logic domain: confirm no pin on IC1 ever exceeds 1.89 V plus 0.5 V. This is the specific check for correction 10.
N Fit U1. Confirm the AFE responds over SPI and that VREF settles to its specified value with the capacitor returned to AGND.
N Calibrate against R7 alone with no electrodes attached. Absolute accuracy comes from here.
N Measure the frequency response of the sense path with a known resistive load to characterise the R5 and R6 pole before trusting any impedance above 100 kHz.
N Measure the DLPF step response at a frequency step to confirm the 50 ms per point budget is real. See section 8.
N Only then connect electrodes, and only on a battery, with no debug probe attached.
